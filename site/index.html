    Things that scare us too

    Scaredy GmCat works differently from normal file sharing. Your file stays on your device and the other person gets it from your open page. So:

    • Keep this page open until they have it. Close it, lose your internet, or let your computer sleep, and the link vanishes. You'd need to make a new one.
    • On a phone, keep the screen on and stay on this page. Phones pause pages in the background, which stops the transfer. A computer works best for sending.
    • They must download while you're online. It's not "send now, grab it later". Agree on a time, or send the link when you're both around.
    • If the connection drops partway, it carries on. Scaredy reconnects by itself and the file picks up where it stopped, as long as both pages stay open. A half-finished file is never saved as if it were complete.
    • Refreshing is fine. Your files and your link come back by themselves, and anyone receiving picks up again. Closing the page is different: the link vanishes, and the copy this browser kept of your files is wiped automatically the next time Scaredy GmCat is opened in this browser, once at least half an hour has passed (your own files on your device aren't touched).
    • Very big files (several GB) go straight into the other person's downloads as they arrive. On an iPhone or iPad they may still be too much for the browser.
    • The secret is in the link. Your file is locked with a key that's part of the link itself (the bit after the "#"). Browsers never send that part to any server, so not even our relay can open your file. It also means anyone with the whole link can get the file while you're sharing, so send it only to the person it's for. (A Short link has no room for the key, so it passes through our server while you connect; we never save it.)

        Questions

        Is it really never stored?

        Yes. In Direct mode the file goes from your browser to theirs and our server only helps the two browsers find each other. In Encrypted relay mode (or when Direct is blocked and that person is switched to the relay), locked pieces pass through our relay and are handed over and forgotten one by one. Nothing is written to disk.

        Can you see my file?

        With Direct and Encrypted relay, no: it's encrypted in your browser with a key that lives in the part of the link after "#", which browsers never send to any server, ours included. With Short link the key passes through our server while you share (never saved). The file still goes straight between you, unless their network blocks that and you choose to send theirs through our relay: then our server holds both the key and the locked pieces while they pass. We never save or open either, but if you want zero trust in us, use Direct or Encrypted relay.

        Why didn't it connect?

        Some networks (offices, schools, some mobile carriers) block direct browser-to-browser connections. With Direct, Scaredy switches that person to the encrypted relay by itself (unless you untick that), so you don't need a new link. With a Short link it never switches by itself: our server holds a Short link's key, so a button lets you choose to use the relay for that person, knowing you're trusting us a little more.

        Can I send to several people?

        Yes. Send the same link to each of them. Each person gets their own copy while your page is open, and you can watch each one's progress.

        Can I send several files, or add one later?

        Yes. Pick as many as you like. While the link is ready you can add more files or remove some, and anyone with the link sees the list change straight away.

        Which option should I pick?

        Direct is the most private. If it can't connect, or you'd rather not show your internet address, use Encrypted relay. Pick Short link when you need a link that's easy to type or say out loud; the trade-off is that the secret key passes through our server while you connect.

        What if I refresh the page?

        Nothing is lost. While you share, the page keeps a copy of your files in your own browser (it never leaves your device), so after a refresh your files and your link come straight back, and anyone receiving reconnects by themselves. A file that was halfway across carries on where it stopped. The copy is wiped when you press Stop sharing. If you close the page instead, the link vanishes after a few seconds, and the copy is wiped automatically the next time Scaredy GmCat is opened in this browser, once at least half an hour has passed.

        Is there a size limit?

        Not for Direct. The relay has a fair-use limit of 6 GB per link. In practice the other person's browser decides how big is too big; several GB can be a stretch on phones.

        Do I need an account?

        No. Nobody signs up, nobody signs in.

        Can I check the code?

        Yes. The code behind Scaredy GmCat is public on GitHub, so anyone can check how files are locked, what our server does (and doesn't) keep, and run it themselves.
