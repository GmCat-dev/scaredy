    Things that scare us too

    Scaredy GmCat works differently from normal file sharing. Your file stays on your device and the other person gets it from your open page. So:

    • Keep this page open until they have it. Close it, lose your internet, or let your computer sleep, and the link vanishes. You'd need to make a new one.
    • On a phone, keep the screen on and stay on this page. Phones pause pages in the background, which stops the transfer. A computer works best for sending.
    • They must download while you're online. It's not "send now, grab it later". Agree on a time, or send the link when you're both around.
    • If the connection drops partway, they start again. Nothing is kept halfway, anywhere.
    • Refreshing is fine. Your files and your link come back by themselves, and anyone receiving picks up again. Closing the page is different: the link vanishes.
    • Very big files (several GB) may be too much for the other person's browser, especially on a phone.
    • The secret is in the link. Your file is locked with a key that's part of the link itself (the bit after the "#"). Browsers never send that part to any server, so not even our relay can open your file. It also means anyone with the whole link can get the file while you're sharing, so send it only to the person it's for. (A Short link has no room for the key, so it passes through our server while you connect; we never save it.)

        Questions

        Is it really never stored?

        Yes. In Direct mode the file goes from your browser to theirs and our server only helps the two browsers find each other. In Encrypted relay mode, locked pieces pass through our relay and are handed over and forgotten one by one. Nothing is written to disk.

        Can you see my file?

        With Direct and Encrypted relay, no: it's encrypted in your browser with a key that lives in the part of the link after "#", which browsers never send to any server, ours included. With Short link the key passes through our server while the browsers connect. We never save it and the file still goes straight between you, but if you want zero trust in us, use Direct or Encrypted relay.

        Why didn't it connect?

        Some networks (offices, schools, some mobile carriers) block direct browser-to-browser connections. Make a new link with Encrypted relay; it works on any network.

        Can I send to several people?

        Yes. Send the same link to each of them. Each person gets their own copy while your page is open, and you can watch each one's progress.

        Can I send several files, or add one later?

        Yes. Pick as many as you like. While the link is ready you can add more files or remove some, and anyone with the link sees the list change straight away.

        Which option should I pick?

        Direct is the most private. If it can't connect, or you'd rather not show your internet address, use Encrypted relay. Pick Short link when you need a link that's easy to type or say out loud; the trade-off is that the secret key passes through our server while you connect.

        What if I refresh the page?

        Nothing is lost. While you share, the page keeps a copy of your files in your own browser (it never leaves your device), so after a refresh your files and your link come straight back, and anyone receiving reconnects by themselves. A file that was halfway across starts again. The copy is wiped when you press Stop sharing. If you close the page instead, the link vanishes after a few seconds and the copy is wiped the next time you open Scaredy GmCat.

        Is there a size limit?

        Not for Direct. The relay has a fair-use limit of 6 GB per link. In practice the other person's browser decides how big is too big; several GB can be a stretch on phones.

        Do I need an account?

        No. Nobody signs up, nobody signs in.
